# Brand Kit: Medallion

> Medallion's tone of voice and key phrases from their own copy.
> Source: context/docs/research/voice.md, https://medallion.co | Checked: 2026-11-13

---

## Tone of Voice

**Confident and direct**
Medallion states metrics and outcomes upfront without hedging. "Better outcomes for credentialing. Faster paths to billable providers." Not: "We help improve credentialing processes for better outcomes."

**Business-outcome focused**
Leads with revenue and efficiency impact, not feature descriptions. "2x faster time-to-revenue" before "AI agents that submit applications." The benefit comes before the mechanism.

**Mission-driven undercurrent**
Operations work is tied to patient access and care outcomes. "Get providers back to care." "See patients sooner." The compliance and efficiency narrative connects back to real people waiting for care.

**Not clinical**
Writes for operations and revenue leaders, not clinicians or credentialing specialists. Avoids clinical jargon. Uses business terms: "time-to-billing," "administrative lag," "revenue driver."

**Measured urgency**
Creates urgency through financial framing ("every day a provider can't bill is revenue you're not collecting") rather than fear or compliance threats. Urgency feels like a business decision, not a scare tactic.

**Empathetic toward patients (undercurrent)**
References patients who are "low-income, elderly, immunocompromised, or lack transportation" in thought leadership. This is background tone, not headline copy.

## Writing Rules (Derived from Medallion's Voice)

1. Lead with the outcome, follow with the mechanism
2. Use specific metrics when available; don't substitute vague adjectives ("faster" with no number)
3. Write for the operations and revenue leader, not the credentialing specialist
4. Connect operational efficiency to patient access in thought leadership; keep it implicit in cold outreach
5. No em dashes (consistent with CLAUDE.md rule)
6. Never use "best-in-class" or "industry-leading" without a specific, sourced metric attached
7. Urgency through business math, not compliance fear (unless writing for CCO/GC persona)
8. Short sentences. Direct claims. No filler phrases like "I wanted to reach out" or "I hope this finds you well."

## What Medallion Does NOT Sound Like

- Overly technical ("our NCQA-aligned credentialing verification workflow leverages machine learning models...")
- Vague and corporate ("we're committed to transforming healthcare operations for a better tomorrow")
- Fear-based compliance marketing ("one lapsed license could expose you to $50,000 in CMS penalties") — this is for CCO persona only, not general brand voice
- Humble ("we think we might be able to help with...") — Medallion is confident and direct
