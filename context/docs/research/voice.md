## Key phrases and language

**Problem framing:**
- "Fire-drill compliance" — reactive, after-the-fact compliance work
- "Administrative lag" — licensing and enrollment delays that slow growth
- "Time-to-billing" — the revenue gap between provider onboarding and first billable claim
- "Labyrinthine", "red tape", "bottlenecks", "snail pace" — describes manual credentialing process
- "Inadvertently running afoul of state regulations" — compliance risk language

**Outcome framing:**
- "Get providers back to care"
- "See patients sooner"
- "Effortless credentialing"
- "Scale efficiently without increasing headcount"
- "Stay compliant proactively rather than reactively"
- "Faster paths to billable providers"
- "Turn provider onboarding into a predictable revenue driver"
- "Unlock revenue 2x faster" (self-reported metric)
- "66% reduction in administrative costs" (self-reported metric)
- "99.9% average file accuracy" (self-reported metric)
- "Credentialing files ready in about one day on average" (self-reported metric)

**Proof language:**
- "$2.4M in ROI" (Tampa General case study)
- "Cut credentialing time by 80%"
- "Slashed credentialing denials by 50%"
- "Scaled provider onboarding across 700+ clinics without adding headcount"
- "Performance-backed guarantees written into contracts"

**Mission language:**
- "Reimagining provider operations"
- "Delivering better outcomes at lower costs"
- "Healthcare automation to improve patients' lives"
- Empathetic patient framing: "patients who are low-income, elderly, immunocompromised, or lack transportation"

---

## Tone

- **Confident and direct:** States metrics and guarantees upfront
- **Business-outcome focused:** Leads with revenue and efficiency, not features
- **Mission-driven undercurrent:** Ties operations work to patient access and care
- **Not clinical:** Writes for operations and revenue leaders, not clinicians
- **Measured urgency:** "Now" framing without panic; positions inaction as a choice that costs money

---

## Words they avoid / would not use

- Overly technical/clinical jargon (they write for ops leaders, not credentialing specialists)
- Em dashes (not observed in their copy)
- Vague platitudes like "best-in-class" or "industry-leading" without proof (they prefer specific metrics)
