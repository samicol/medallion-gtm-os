## Key market drivers (why companies are buying now)

### 1. Telehealth expansion post-pandemic
- Telehealth is approaching 20% of outpatient and office visit claims (Medallion blog, unverified externally)
- Each telehealth provider must be licensed in every state where the patient is located — not just where the provider practices
- Multi-state licensing requirements multiply credentialing volume per provider dramatically
- Companies hiring 10, 50, or 100 virtual providers face 50x the licensing work of a single-state group
Source: https://medallion.co/resources/blog/breaking-through-licensing-credentialing-bottlenecks-for-telehealth-providers | Checked: 2026-11-13
Source: https://www.credentially.io/blogs/telehealth-credentialing-in-2025-trends-challenges-and-opportunities | Checked: 2026-11-13

### 2. CMS regulatory tightening
- CMS issued updated telehealth credentialing guidance requiring standardized license verification for every state served (year of issuance unconfirmed — verify at CMS.gov before citing)
- One source cites a requirement to review every provider credentialing file every 30 days for all files processed after July 1, 2025 — verify against CMS or accreditor primary sources before relying on this
- CMS can impose penalties of $10,000–$50,000 per violation (unverified figure — confirm against Federal Register)
Source: https://247medicalbillingservices.com/blog/preparing-for-2025-how-new-regulations-impact-credentialing | Checked: 2026-11-13

### 3. Interstate licensing compacts expanding
- **Interstate Medical Licensure Compact (IMLC):** Streamlines physician licensing across member states; a provider licensed in one IMLC state can get expedited licenses in others
- **Nurses Licensure Compact (NLC):** Similar compact for nurses
- Compacts create urgency: companies that haven't pursued compact licensing are leaving revenue on the table, since competitors can staff providers in more states faster
Source: https://medwave.io/2025/02/the-future-of-provider-credentialing-trends-and-predictions/ | Checked: 2026-11-13

### 4. Digital health funding cycle creating hiring waves
- Digital health companies that raise Series A/B rounds typically accelerate provider hiring immediately after
- More providers hired = more credentialing and licensing applications = more operational strain
- Manual credentialing (spreadsheets, email) breaks at ~20–30 providers; above that threshold, companies must systematize
INFERRED from Medallion's customer base and product positioning

### 5. Healthcare workforce shortage driving staffing expansion
- Physician shortage is projected to reach 86,000 physicians by 2036 (AAMC, cited by Medallion blog)
- Health systems and digital health companies are expanding their geographic footprints to recruit across state lines
- Expanding geographic footprint = more state licenses needed per provider
Source: https://medallion.co/resources/blog/breaking-through-licensing-credentialing-bottlenecks-for-telehealth-providers | Checked: 2026-11-13

### 6. Revenue risk from enrollment delays
- Payer enrollment takes 60–120 days on average (industry-cited figure, verify before using)
- During that window, providers cannot bill; organizations carry the salary cost with no revenue return
- For a 10-provider group at $200K average salary: a 90-day enrollment delay = ~$500K in lost revenue (INFERRED calculation)
- This financial pain is what Medallion calls "time-to-billing" and uses as a core selling point

## Urgency factors (creates NOW vs. LATER buying behavior)

| Trigger | Why it creates urgency |
|---|---|
| New state expansion | Providers can't see patients or bill until licensed + enrolled in that state |
| New provider hire | Same — billing clock starts at hire, not at licensure |
| Funding round closed | Investor milestone pressure; growth plans require rapid headcount scaling |
| Compliance audit or denial | Reactive — creates immediate need to fix credentialing gaps |
| Manual process breaking down | Usually at 20–50 providers; spreadsheet management fails |
| New payer contract signed | Enrollment must complete before first billable claim |
