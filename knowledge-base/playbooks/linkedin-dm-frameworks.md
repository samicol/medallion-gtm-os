# LinkedIn DM Frameworks

## When to Use

- Running LinkedIn outreach via a LinkedIn automation tool (e.g. HeyReach) alongside or instead of email
- Prospect is more reachable on LinkedIn than email (enterprise, C-suite)
- Signal source is LinkedIn-native (content engagement, mutual connections, profile changes)
- Multi-channel campaigns where LinkedIn is a parallel touch

> **Shared foundations:** The 14 Iron Laws, SPARK framework, 6 copy archetypes, and the conditional waterfall all still apply. See `knowledge-base/playbooks/copy-iron-laws.md` and `knowledge-base/playbooks/copy-frameworks.md`. This document covers ONLY what differs on LinkedIn.

---

## What Changes vs. Cold Email

| Dimension | Cold Email | LinkedIn DM |
|-----------|------------|-------------|
| **Entry** | Direct to inbox | Connection request first (empty, no note) |
| **Length** | 60-120 words | DM 1: one sentence; DM 2: 2-3 sentences |
| **Subject line** | Critical (3-5 words, lowercase) | Does not exist |
| **Greeting** | `{{first_name}},` | Omit or just first name, it's chat |
| **Signature** | `{{sender_signature}}` with title | `{{sender_name}}`, no title block |
| **CTA** | Low-friction question | Even softer, observation or curiosity |
| **Tone** | Conversational email | Pure chat, shorter sentences, more informal |
| **Follow-ups** | 3-4 steps over 14 days | 2 DMs only, over 5-7 days |
| **Links** | OK in body | Avoid in first touch (LinkedIn suppresses link-heavy messages) |
| **PS lines** | Common (bait, incentive, credential) | Rare, use only when high-value (incentive offers) |
| **Sending tool** | Your email sequencer (e.g. Smartlead, Instantly, Lemlist) | Your LinkedIn automation tool (e.g. HeyReach) |

## The LinkedIn Touch Structure

### Touch 0: Connection Request

**Default: Empty, no note.** Send the connection request without a message.

Why: Connection request notes are almost always worse than no note. They either sound like a pitch or like generic flattery. An empty request with a relevant profile (title, headline, mutual connections) converts better than a note that screams "I'm about to sell you something."

Exception: Only add a note for warm signals (champion migration, stakeholder reactivation, content engagement) where you have a genuine personal reason to connect.

### Touch 1: First DM (1-2 days after accepted)

**One question. That's it.** A forward-looking question tied to their situation, something easy enough that a stranger would actually answer.

Rules:
- One sentence max. Reads like a text message.
- A question related to their situation (not a pitch disguised as a question)
- **Must be answerable to a stranger.** Don't ask sensitive/strategic questions (pipeline health, revenue numbers, team performance). Nobody tells a stranger how their pipeline is going. Ask forward-looking questions: plans, initiatives, direction. Yes/no or either/or questions work best.
- No greeting ("Thanks for connecting" wastes space)
- No signature block
- No explanation of who you are

Examples:
- "{{first_name}}, any initiatives to revamp your outbound as part of your new role?"
- "{{first_name}}, are you building outbound workflows in Clay or using it more for enrichment?"
- "{{first_name}}, any plans to scale outbound post-raise?"

### Touch 2: Context + Soft CTA (3-5 days after Touch 1)

**Add context that makes your question make sense.** Still brief, 2-3 sentences max.

Rules:
- Reference your DM 1 question ("Asking because...")
- One proof point or relevant context
- Soft offer, not a pitch
- Still reads like a text, not an email

Examples:
- "Asking because we usually build 6 for companies at your stage, happy to show you what that looks like if useful."
- "We see most teams at 1-2 workflows. The ones growing fastest are running 6+. Happy to share what that looks like."

**No Touch 3.** Two DMs is the limit. If they don't reply after DM 2, move on. Don't chase on LinkedIn.

## Connection Request Formulas

These are the exception, not the default (empty requests win; see Touch 0). When a note is justified, pick based on campaign type and signal:

### Signal-Based Connection Requests

**Hiring signal:**
```
{{first_name}}, seems you're scaling the engineering team at {{company_name}}.
Would love to connect, we work with similar companies on [topic].
```

**New leadership:**
```
{{first_name}}, good to see the move into the {{job_title}} role. Always good to connect
with leaders building [function] at growing companies.
```

**Funding signal:**
```
{{first_name}}, the recent round is great news. Interesting to follow
how {{company_name}} scales from here. Happy to connect.
```

### Bait Connection Requests

**Form D / Market intel:**
```
{{first_name}}, found some interesting data on companies in [niche]
announcing funding. Thought it might be useful. Happy to connect.
```

### Niche-Specific Connection Requests

**Industry peer:**
```
{{first_name}}, building [function] at [stage] companies is no joke.
Would love to follow the journey at {{company_name}}.
```

### Warm Connection Requests (Past Relationship)

**Champion migration:**
```
{{first_name}}, we worked with {{previous_company}} back in {{year}}.
Great to see you at {{new_company_name}}, wanted to reconnect.
```

**Stakeholder reactivation:**
```
{{first_name}}, it's been a while since we worked with {{company_name}}.
Wanted to stay connected.
```

**Content engagement:**
```
{{first_name}}, your post on [topic] resonated, especially the point
about [specific detail]. Happy to connect.
```

## How Conditional Formatting Adapts for LinkedIn

The same signal waterfall from email applies, but compressed:

**Email version (Line 1 + Line 2 = 3-5 sentences):**
```
+ FUNDRAISING: {{first_name}}, I bet investors are expecting pipeline to
grow since your recent raise. That's why you're spending $800 on Clay's
Pro Plan. But from what I can see, there are no GTM engineers on your team yet.
Who's building your workflows to make the most of those 50k credits?
```

**LinkedIn version (same signals, 1-2 sentences):**
```
+ FUNDRAISING: now that the round is closed, if pipeline expectations
are ramping up and you need help with Clay workflows, let me know , 
that's exactly what we do.
```

**Rules for LinkedIn compression:**
- Keep the signal reference (it proves homework)
- Cut the elaboration (no room for 3-sentence signal observations)
- Merge value prop into the same breath as the observation
- CTA can be implicit ("let me know") rather than explicit ("Can I send it over?")

## Priority Sequencing

When running LinkedIn alongside email, prioritize plays in this order:

| Priority | Play | Why LinkedIn First |
|----------|------|-------------------|
| 1 | Champion Migration | Warmest signal, past relationship at new company. LinkedIn = peer reconnection. |
| 2 | Stakeholder Reactivation | Known contact, re-engaging. LinkedIn feels more personal than cold email. |
| 3 | New Leadership | A new-role message on LinkedIn is natural. In cold email it can feel forced. |
| 4 | Content Engagement | Signal is LinkedIn-native. Respond where the signal happened. |
| 5 | Competitor Customers | LinkedIn profile shows competitor relationship. Reference naturally. |
| 6 | Hiring Intent | Generic signal, email is usually the primary channel here. |

For plays at priority 1-3, LinkedIn can be the **primary** channel. For 4-6, LinkedIn supplements email.

## Multi-Channel Coordination

When running both email and LinkedIn for the same campaign:

1. **Stagger timing:** If email goes Day 0, LinkedIn connection request goes Day 1-2
2. **Different angles per channel:** Email leads with the observation, LinkedIn leads with the question
3. **No content duplication:** LinkedIn DMs should NOT repeat email copy verbatim
4. **Pause on reply:** If they reply on one channel, pause the other immediately
5. **LinkedIn before outreach:** Engage with their content (like/comment) 2-3 days before sending the connection request
6. **Volume limits:** 20-30 connection requests per day max per account

## Anti-Patterns

Things that kill LinkedIn outreach:

- **Pitching in the connection request**, instant reject. The request is for relevance, not revenue.
- **200-word DMs**, it's chat, not email. If you're writing paragraphs, switch to email.
- **"I came across your profile"**, everyone says this. It signals zero homework.
- **Links in first DM**, LinkedIn suppresses messages with URLs. Save links for Touch 2+.
- **InMail templates that read like email**, different medium, different norms. InMail should still feel like a DM.
- **Automated generic connection requests**, "I'd love to add you to my network" = auto-reject for decision makers.
- **Sending to people who haven't accepted**, respect the connection boundary. If they don't accept after 2 weeks, they're not interested.

## Adapting the 6 Copy Archetypes for LinkedIn

Each archetype from `knowledge-base/playbooks/copy-frameworks.md` works on LinkedIn with compression:

### The Observation → LinkedIn
- **Connection request:** The observation itself (signal reference)
- **DM 1:** "Connected because [observation]. [One line about implication]. How are you approaching it?"
- **DM 2:** Social proof from similar company

### Problem/Solution → LinkedIn
- **Connection request:** Mention the problem space
- **DM 1:** Name the problem in one sentence. One proof point. "Worth chatting about?"
- **DM 2:** Resource or benchmark

### The Referral Ceiling → LinkedIn
- **Connection request:** Peer connection in their growth stage
- **DM 1:** Acknowledge their growth channel. Name the ceiling. "Is this on your radar?"
- **DM 2:** How similar companies broke through

### Creative Ideas → LinkedIn
- **Connection request:** Reference something specific about their business
- **DM 1:** "Put together [X] ideas for {{company_name}}. Mind if I share?"
- **DM 2:** Share the ideas directly

### The Benchmark → LinkedIn
- **Connection request:** Industry peer / data-sharing framing
- **DM 1:** One benchmark data point. "Want to see where {{company_name}} lands?"
- **DM 2:** Offer to share the full analysis

### The Whole Offer → LinkedIn
- **Connection request:** Credibility-based (mutual connections, shared experience)
- **DM 1:** One-sentence credential. One result. "Relevant for {{company_name}}?"
- **DM 2:** Case study or walkthrough offer
